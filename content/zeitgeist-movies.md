## Zeitgeist: The Movie

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The first film, [*Zeitgeist: The Movie*](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OrHeg77LF4Y), blew my mind. It talks about everything from world religions to 9/11 conspiracies to technology to the U.S. monetary system.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->

### Part I - The Greatest Story Ever Told (0:13:12)

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The first part is an interesting, intellectual dissection of religions. It explains why most religions have the same allegorical stories - stories that involve the zodiac and movements of the Sun. This part also introduces the idea of the religious myth as the most powerful myth in the history of human beings, and why it is the basis upon which other (i.e. political) myths can flourish.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->

### Part II - All the World's a Stage (0:40:15)

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The second part was all about September 11, 2001, aka 9/11, and the conspiracy theories surrounding the events that happened that day. It brings up the idea that 9/11 was a "false-flag" operation: the government acting against its citizens with the intent of deflecting blame and inflaming citizens to act against an accused.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

It felt like the delivery and the message had changed from the first part. Maybe Peter was trying to invoke an emotional response after Part I butters-up viewers with intellectualism. Whatever his reason, he changed tack in Part II.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

I think the point of Part II was to make the connection between the religious myth (most powerful, basis for other myths) and the power that governments have to perpetuate mythical misconceptions to mislead citizens away from the truth. I think the connection between the parts is too subtle. I didn't make the connection the first time I viewed it.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->

### Part III - Don't Mind the Men Behind the Curtain (1:16:00)

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The third and last part is vague. It looked like it was unrefined, unfinished, not well put together. It introduces the idea of the central bank and the flawed monetary system. (See "[The American Dream Film](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ExBE651_vOY)" on YouTube, by [theamericandreamfilm](http://www.youtube.com/user/theamericandreamfilm))

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Part 3 also introduces the concept of the "invisible government" which is behind the front government. It owes no allegiance nor responsibility to anyone (Theodore Roosevelt said this). The invisible government consists of powerful international bankers that virtually run the US government for their own purposes (John Hylan, Mayor of New York City, said this). And, Franklin Roosevelt acknowledged that a financial element has owned the government since Andrew Jackson.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The invisible government is the Federal Reserve Bank. Part 3 describes how the Fed was created and how it controls the money supply. It also spells out how Federal Income Tax - a tax that was never legally ratified by the states - came into being: the means by which we pay for the government's loan of money from the Fed.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

One more concept introduced in the first film is that war is good for (bank) business because it forces people to borrow more money (at interest) plus the finance of military production. World War I netted an estimated \$16 Billion profit.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

In World War II, the United States only entered the war after we allowed Pearl Harbor to get attacked - if we look at it through conspiracy goggles. Then, U.S. companies (e.g. Rockefeller's Standard Oil) funded both sides of the war effort.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

A final point of the film is that our government keeps us (the citizens) entertained and undereducated so that we don't stand-up or balk at its actions.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Peter says, "The last thing the power establishment wants is a conscience, informed public capable of critical thinking. This is why a continually fraudulent zeitgeist is output via religion, the mass media and the educational system. It is in their interest to keep you in a distracted, naive bubble. And they are doing a damn good job of it."

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

If this is true, then this blog is 90% rehashing of what I've been fed in the media. I don't think that is the case. I think (some) citizens know what's going on and they report it to blogs or the media or to anyone who will listen.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

I thought the first film had a mixed message: religion is a myth and governments perpetuate fear against citizens. I don't think I understood the point or how it sets up the second movie.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

I guess the setup is the last thing Peter says on the film, "However...if people ever realize the truth of their relationship to nature and the truth of their personal power to affect change...the entire manufactured zeitgeist that’s preyed upon...would collapse like a house of cards."

## Zeitgeist: Addendum

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The second movie, [***Zeitgeist: Addendum***](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EewGMBOB4Gg), drills down on the problems spelled out in the first film.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Part I ()

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

In the beginning of the second film Peter mentions the book [Modern Money Mechanics](http://teamsiems.com/ModernMoneyMechanics.pdf). I think it is important to read this book before we make too many judgments. I also think you should know that Peter was a private equity trader before he became a film maker.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The book describes (and Peter summarizes) how money works in America - specifically it describes the fractional reserve system. The government borrows money from the Fed (a bank), deposits it into another bank, and distributes the money to the reserve banks. So money is created out of debt and debt is a form of slavery.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The fractional part relates to the reserve requirements of banks in this country: they must keep 10% of their total money in reserve and the rest is free to lend. However, Peter says, the government doesn't actually distribute the 90%, rather, that amount is created out of thin air. But, once received, the 90% "funny money" is loaned out to people and companies (minus 10%), and thus it trickles down to every other bank.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Part II ()

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

All of this debt and interest and economic slavery lead to the creation of the "economic hitman." The hitman theory goes like this. International banks like the IMF or World Bank, arrange a huge loan to a country that has natural resources (like oil), but the money goes to big companies to build the infrastructure. Then the country is left with repaying the debt. Of course they can't repay so then the big companies (oil) come an make deals for the natural resources as greatly reduced prices.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

We've done this with several countries and thus we begun setting up an empire. The emperor of which is the "corporatocracy" or the big corporations. They control media. They control politicians. They maximize profit regardless of the social and environmental cost. There is a name for this and it is "globalization."

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Anyone who challenges this way of operation is labeled a terrorist, and thereby, they can be persecuted as such.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

So the corporatocracy is doing the same thing to the world that the Fed does to America.

This made me think that America could be vulnerable to "reverse globalization." In other words we might loose social programs (health care) and other nationally vital infrastructure could be privatized - further enslaving us to other countries, like China.

Part I - Human Nature (0:09:10)

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Again, this film and this part take a strange tack away from money and resources. In this first part Peter introduces us to "nature vs. nurture" concepts; our behavior isn't predetermined but programmed during our lives. It's the idea that environment determines behavior.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

This part concludes with a hint of the first part of the first film: religion - or lack of religion - can play a role in influencing our behavior. We are shaped by society; one part of that being religion. We (the US) are also the most stratified, individualistic societies, and with capitalism as our economic model, we fight to climb the later to success and isolate ourselves even more. If you fail there's no safety net - you're done. Societies like that are very unlikely to be altruistic. The myth is that this is in our nature. The opposite is true: it is environment and society that drives behavior.

Part II - Social Pathology (0:42:11)

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

(The Market 42:30)

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

This section begins with Dr. John McMurtry asking, "Where did this all begin? This state of cumulative collapse?" He says it starts with John Locke: if you provide labor, you are entitled to the product, but money nullifies this idea. People can buy labor with money. There is no longer a consideration if there is enough left over for others. There is no longer a consideration if it spoils because money is like gold and gold can't spoil.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Adam Smith, adding to Locke, in the Wealth of Nations, says equilibrium of supply and demand comes from the invisible hand of the market. Later Smith says something like "with scarcity, children will die off; in abundance children will live - this is the invisible hand (of God)."

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Peter comes in a reminds us that money is a commodity. We trade it like we trade goods and services. He also get McMurtry to explain "Money Sequence of Value" versus "Life Sequence of Value."

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Next Peter introduces the terms "Intrinsic Obsolescence" and "Planned Obsolescence" which means making products so that they need to be replaced. This concept leads to the conclusion that efficiency and advancements - e.g. curing cancer would cost jobs - are bad, and crime is good - e.g. need more police, jails, etc..

(Monetary System 1:09:16)

Life expectancy is inversely proportional to income inequality; drug abuse is directly proportional. He calls this "Psychosocial Stress." And its cause is the (hybrid) Monetary-Market System.

Part III - Project Earth (1:30:05)

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Peter starts this section with us assessing Earth's resources and tracking our use of these resources This leads to a <span style="text-decoration: underline;">global resource management system</span>. After we know what we have and how fast we use it, how do we optimize production/refinement of these resources? This is what I call resource deference spectroscopy or quantifying the return on investment (ROI) versus the (health) risks of using one resource over another; he calls it avoiding negative retroactions.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Then he introduces strategies: *strategic preservation*, *strategic safety*, *strategic efficiency* (goods are designed to last, designed to recycle, designed to update). The implementation of these strategies leads to a <span style="text-decoration: underline;">global production management system</span>.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

After identifying resources and our rate of use to optimize their production, he asks what about distribution. Using the "proximity strategy" goods would be produced as close as possible to where they are needed. Do you see a pattern? That's right global distribution system which he calls a <span style="text-decoration: underline;">demand & distribution tracking system</span>. This would insure needs are met.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Then comes the idea of *strategic access* or use of goods when they're needed. This is converse to ownership which lets the good sit idle when we don't use it; a waste.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

All of this leads to a **resource-based economy** (see <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacque_Fresco" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Jacque Fresco</a>) or "the scientific method applied to social concern."

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Next he proposes the city of the future. It would be circular with areas for food, production, residential, power, recreation, social, and education. This reminded me of [EPCOT](http://disneyworld.disney.go.com/parks/epcot/) (Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow) at Disney World.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

How is the city made? Robots. Machines that make machines and service humans with a diminishing rate of failure.

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Peter also "answers" the question of - "so what happens when people have nothing to do?" Will we turn into fat lazy blobs? "No", he says. Our curious, innovative being will reemerge. We'll do things simply for the reward of creating something new, of value, that helps society.
